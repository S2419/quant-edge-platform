What a league move does to a player's shots

It depends which way he goes. Stepping up costs shots. Moving across makes no clear difference.

Corrected on 25 September 2026. Figures updated after a data fix. One reading changed: the small rise for players dropping down a division now just clears zero. Details at the end.

  • Second division to a top league: about 10% fewer shots per 90. Somewhere between 6% and 14% fewer, on 266 moves.
  • Smaller top league to the big five: about 18% fewer. Between 9% and 27% fewer, on 61 moves. The Eredivisie, Portugal, Belgium and Scotland into England, Spain, Italy, Germany or France.
  • Between top leagues: no clear change. The average is 2% fewer, and the range runs from 8% fewer to 4% more. This group also includes moves from the big five down to a smaller top league.
  • Down to a second division: a few more. About 6% more, somewhere between 0.5% and 12% more. Small, and only just clear of zero, so treat it as tentative.
  • We checked it on 442 moves into 2025/26 that the fit never saw. It held for moves across and down. For stepping up, our ranges were too narrow, so we don't give one.

Shots per 90 minutes in league football. A move is an attacker or midfielder who played a season in one of our 14 divisions and the next season in a different one, loans included. Only players who took five or more shots after the move are in the averages, so this is about players who got a game: 1,262 is every move we found, the averages use the 773 moves into 2023/24 and 2024/25 that clear that bar, and the test uses all 442 moves into 2025/26.

Compared with what?

The obvious check is this season against last season. That mixes two things up: the new league, and the fact that a player coming off a big season usually drops back wherever he plays next.

So we compare each mover with what we'd have expected if he'd stayed in his old league. Whatever gap is left is the move.

Second division to a top league26610% fewer6% to 14% fewer

Top league down to a second division2236% more0.5% to 12% more

The step into the big five from a smaller league is the biggest effect and the least certain one, because there were only 61 such moves in two seasons. The step up from a second division is smaller but much firmer.

Did the ranges hold up?

For each player we also give an 80% range for his shots per 90 after the move. Say 80% and it should happen about 80% of the time. We fitted everything on moves into 2023/24 and 2024/25, then checked it against 442 moves into 2025/26.

Between top leagues121100 (83%)Holds

The average for stepping up is solid. The spread around it isn't. Some players step up and shoot more than ever, some disappear, and our range doesn't stretch far enough to cover both. So for that move we give the average and no range.

We need at least 100 tested moves before we call a range reliable, so the step into the big five stays uncalled for now, even though 84% landed inside it.

What this doesn't cover

  • Finishing. How a player's conversion changes after a move hasn't been tested, so we don't translate it.
  • Shot quality. This is shot volume only. We don't have shot locations or xG for these leagues.
  • Loans and permanent moves together. We now have transfer records that can split them, but this study doesn't yet.
